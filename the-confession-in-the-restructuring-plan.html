The Confession in the Restructuring Plan

What the plan to cut the solution engineer role says about how the role was designed, and how to redesign it before it is cut.

Brandon Freitag

The book’s first line

Somewhere in your organization, a conversation is happening that you are not part of.

You have seen the signs

The evidence is already in the room.

The headcount freeze
It was framed as doing more with less.
The AI demo tool
It was introduced as a productivity improvement.
The reorg
It raised coverage ratios.
What the plan says
The restructuring plan, in one sentence

The solution engineer role is expensive, inconsistent, and increasingly handled by AI.

Read it as a diagnostician

It is an accurate description of the role the company built.

What the role was measured on
Demos delivered
Proofs of concept completed
Pipeline influenced

You replace a tool with a better tool.

What the plan confesses

The plan is right about the role, and wrong about what the role could be.

Move 2 of 5 · The gap

Success is declared at signature. The outcome is measured later.

Enterprise technology projects have missed their intended outcomes at a stubborn rate, through shelf after shelf of sales methodology.

The signature
Implementation is declared successful.
The gap
The outcome
It is measured later, by someone whose job had not started when the deal closed.
Move 2 of 5 · Who stands in the gap

The SE is the one person in the deal oriented, at least nominally, toward whether it will work rather than whether it will close.

Move 3 of 5 · The forcing function

AI can build a demo.

Much of the tool model’s visible work is now cheap to replicate.

Move 3 of 5 · The industry’s answer

The forward deployed engineer rebuilds the tool model under a new name.

It puts more technical capability next to the customer. When the constraint is the customer’s organization, more technical capability does not move it.

Move 4 of 5 · The choice

The field guide · Tool 1 of 3

The Win-Rate Ladder

Use it to decide which pursuits deserve your time.

Six rungs, from an RFI or RFP response at the bottom to an engagement aligned to a business outcome the customer has named at the top. Where a pursuit starts on the ladder tells you how much of your time it has earned.

The field guide · Tool 2 of 3

The Organizational Screen

Use it to read whether a company can support the partner model. It was written for an SE weighing an employer, and a leadership team can answer it about itself.

Three of its questions
What happened to the customers who signed eighteen months ago, and who can say specifically?
How is a qualified-out deal treated in pipeline review?
Who was promoted last, and for what?
The field guide · Tool 3 of 3

Breakpoint Detection Questions

Use them to find where a customer’s transformation will fail, before it does. Discovery questions for each of the five breakpoints:

Strategic DisconnectionIncentive FragmentationProcess FrictionThe Technology IllusionMomentum Mirage
For example
Ask each leader on the customer’s side, separately, what this purchase has to achieve. If the answers differ, you have found a strategic disconnection before the signature.
Move 5 of 5 · The asset

The partner model builds the one asset no company owns.

What a tool-model career carries
Almost nothing
Product knowledge dates. The CRM belongs to the employer.
What the partner model builds
A reputation
It is held in other people’s heads, so it carries across a company change.
