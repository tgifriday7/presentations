Point of view

Five things I would want you to remember

On running an Americas pre-sales organisation. Each is a conclusion, the evidence behind it, and the question I would ask to test it at Ping. The operating detail sits in the appendix; all of it is a hypothesis until the data says otherwise.

Brandon Freitag  ·  September 2026

1 of 5

Both sides of the table

What works when a pre-sales organisation scales, and what does not, seen from inside two of them and from the customer's chair.

VMware, 2008 to 2013
Enterprise pre-sales through the high-growth years. Quota exceeded across dozens of enterprise customers as the organisation scaled around me.
Oracle and Google Cloud
Principal sales consultant and enterprise cloud architect on Fortune 500 pursuits at Oracle. At Google Cloud, founded and ran the North America strategic architecture practice: pursuits of $20M-plus, the largest in the billions.
DaVita, the customer side
Senior director running 140 people through two layers at a Fortune 200 healthcare system, across the CIO, the CISO and business leadership, inside HIPAA commitments. I sponsored a major technology investment, allocated the budget and spent my own credibility on it.
The question that tests it here
Where does the field's motion look different from what your customers' executives say they need?
2 of 5

Outcomes win

Pursuits anchored to a customer outcome win at a multiple, and they move the deal off the feature sheet, where a bundle wins.

The portfolio told me where to look
At Google Cloud one class of pursuit was about 20% of the work and 70% of the revenue. I proposed and founded a practice on that evidence.
Measured before scaling
Win-rate research across engagement motions: outcome-anchored pursuits won at roughly 9x the rate of RFI-driven ones. Qualification was rebuilt on that finding, which meant declining work, and the practice held 2x the field win rate against a $10.98B target.
Why it also dilutes the competition
Against Entra inside the E5 contract and Okta's catalogue, a feature comparison is the competitor's home ground. An outcome conversation is not. It is where regulated, hybrid and complex customer identity get decided.
The question that tests it here
Where does the field lose deals the platform should be winning?
3 of 5

Alignment beats enablement

Enablement does not beat an incentive. Outcome selling holds only when sponsorship, structure and metrics point the same way.

What enablement alone bought
A 180-plus principal architect community engaged and 300-plus infrastructure specialists enabled. Adoption stayed the exception, because the incentive rewarded pushing product.
What misalignment costs
At DaVita the incentives were split across four owners: speed, reliability, security and delivery. The outcomes landed, 90% faster environments and 27% lower operating cost, and the design that fought them outlived my tenure. Lesson: diagnose the alignment before accepting the target.
What alignment looks like
A qualification standard agreed with sales and written into the cadence. Variable comp tied to technical wins and POV conversion. Four numbers on the scorecard, including what happened after the signature.
The question that tests it here
Where do Sales and SE incentives point in different directions today?
4 of 5

GRIP: two majors each

Goal and Return are the account executive's majors. Implementation and Platform are the SE's. Neither works without the other.

Goal and Return
What state of the world has to be true for this investment to have been worth it, and what is the problem costing today. The AE owns the sponsor and the economics; the SE makes both concrete.
Implementation and Platform
Can this organisation adopt and sustain what it is buying, and does the whole technical solution solve the problem as scoped. The SE owns both; the AE keeps the sponsor engaged through them. Goal opens the conversation; Platform gets the last word.
The mechanism that makes it a partnership
A shared qualification standard decided in advance, so no deal becomes SE versus rep. Time-boxed POVs with success criteria. SE carrying a number beside bookings in the same review. Partnering this way with sales and account leadership at Google assisted $1.5B to $2B a year in pursuits.
The question that tests it here
What does SE owe your forecast today, and what would you want it to owe?
5 of 5

Worth first

How people are treated is how they treat each other and the customer. Trust is the environment a winning team grows in.

The team
Four technical leaders developed into management and senior roles. Hired and coached my own successor when I moved to found the practice; he has grown it since and remains a close friend. One architect left to pursue ministry, with my support.
The company
The DaVita successor I developed stepped up to senior director and later moved into AI and data governance. Development that only serves the org chart is not development; people can tell the difference, and tenure follows.
The customer
The behaviour customers remember is the honest diagnosis said before signature: not ready, or this solves two of your three problems. A vendor's tooling will never say it. A person with a name attached can, and that is where trust compounds.
The question that tests it here
Who was the last SE promoted here, and what were they recognised for?
Appendix

The operating detail behind the five: what I would measure, and what I would want to validate before proposing anything.

Appendix  ·  1 of 5

The four numbers

Three the business already tracks, and the one that decides retention.

Technical win rate, competitive new logo
Against Okta and Entra, by segment and by competitor.
POV conversion, migration and attach
Self-managed to SaaS, ForgeRock to AIC, and the attach of Protect, Verify, Governance, Privilege and Identity for AI. Two thirds of bookings live here.
Cost per technical win
Capacity comes from qualification, partners and automation before it comes from headcount.
What happened next
Adoption against what was sold, outcome achieved, expansion and retention, cut by pre-sale team. The number a signature never proves.
The measure
Whether the four exist in the CRM today. Start with how often the business-outcome field is filled in.
The question that tests it here
Which of these four does the forecast read today, and who sees post-close outcomes by pre-sale team?
Appendix  ·  2 of 5

Two plays, two scorecards

Migration and attach inside the base and competitive new logo are different motions.

The base is most of the business
A mature identity vendor books most new ACV from existing customers. The risk is destabilising a SAML estate; the reward is retention.
New logo runs into the bundle
You do not beat a bundle on features; you win the use cases it cannot serve and quantify the cost of the gap.
One win rate hides which is broken
Two plays, two scorecards, two kinds of SE hour. Read separately or you fix the wrong one.
The measure
Win rate and POV conversion by play, not blended.
The question that tests it here
What is the split of the Americas number between the base and net-new, and where is the win rate weakest?
Appendix  ·  3 of 5

Capacity: four sources

Headcount is the last source of capacity, not the first.

Qualification, fastest
Declining pursuits that win at a ninth of the rate frees more hours than any tool, and shows up in a quarter.
Leaders' time, measured first
How much of the leaders' week is player-coach today is a question for the data, not a plan. The senior IC track exists here; what it should carry is decided after that answer.
Partners, then automation
Integrators delivering migrations so SE hours go to winning. Then the grind automated: RFx, POC environments, discovery synthesis. The SE organisation running its own agents under the product is the demo.
The measure
Hours freed per SE per quarter, and where they went: to executive discovery and readiness, or to more demos.
The question that tests it here
How much of the leaders' time is player-coach today, and how is it counted?
Appendix  ·  4 of 5

The agentic motion

A specialist pod that graduates, incubated where the references are.

Shape
An overlay of four to six senior ICs, one of whom speaks agent-builder language: MCP, token exchange, agent frameworks. The buyer is platform engineering and the CISO.
Mandate
A discovery workshop that scores whether the organisation can absorb what is built, not only whether it works; eight to twelve lighthouse POVs with auditable success criteria; the play and certification for generalists.
Graduation
The pod's metric is the share of agentic deals run without it, with a named steward measured on twelve-month outcomes.
The measure
Referenceable wins with a named buyer; share of agentic deals run without the pod.
The question that tests it here
Do specialists sit in the regions or globally today, and where should the agentic motion live?
Appendix  ·  5 of 5

The first two quarters

Diagnose, instrument, then move. Everything before the data is a hypothesis.

Days 1 to 30: listen
One-to-ones with every leader, ride-alongs on the top pursuits, deal and POV reviews. Two cheap tests: how often the business-outcome field is filled in, and whether leaders across functions describe what the SE organisation is for the same way.
Days 31 to 60: instrument and agree
The four numbers live in the CRM. The qualification standard agreed with the sales leader. What is funded and the checkpoints agreed with leadership, in writing.
Days 61 to 90: first results
First POV outcomes on the new standard, first certification wave. Then the second quarter is execution.
The measure
What changed by the end of the second quarter, in the four numbers, against checkpoints agreed in the first sixty days.
The question that tests it here
What would make you say this hire was a great decision at twelve months?
Measure first.
Then move.

Every page here is a hypothesis until the data says otherwise.
